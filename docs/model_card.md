# Model Card — Second-Purchase Propensity (v5)

## Status

The v5 pipeline changes the cohort to customers after their first delivered
order and predicts a second delivered purchase within the next 90 days.
The model, metadata and evaluation artifacts are generated by the v5 GitHub
Actions retraining workflow. Final performance claims belong in this model
card only after the v5 workflow passes data validation, training, artifact
verification and publication.

## Intended use

Portfolio demonstration of point-in-time customer propensity analytics.
For each eligible monthly snapshot, rank customers by their probability of
making a second delivered purchase within 90 days.

This is not a live marketplace feed, a causal retention system or a
production-ready customer-contact policy. It must not be used to deny service
or make consequential decisions about customers.

## Cohort and target

A customer enters after the first order has been delivered. The first
prediction snapshot is the first day of the following calendar month.
A snapshot is eligible only when the customer has at least one order
purchased and delivered in the preceding 180 days.

The half-open label window is
\`[snapshot_date, snapshot_date + 90 days)\`:
- \`churned = 0\`: at least one second delivered purchase occurs in the window;
- \`churned = 1\`: no delivered purchase occurs in the window.

The SQL target uses eventual delivery to define a delivered event, but future
records are used only for labels and never for predictors.

## Point-in-time predictors

The feature contract contains 49 columns for recent and prior purchase
activity, lifetime delivered-order count, customer tenure, interpurchase-gap
statistics, simultaneous purchase timestamps, recency, revenue, first-order
basket value and composition, product/category/seller diversity, freight
share, payment method and installments, first-order delivery/review experience,
and rolling basket/payment behaviour.

Historical purchase and delivery timestamps must precede the snapshot.
Review scores are included only when
\`review_answer_timestamp < snapshot_date\`. When orders share an identical
purchase timestamp, a zero-gap share is recorded; undefined positive-gap
statistics remain missing for model imputation.

## Data audit and minimum training population

The connected Olist source audit found 99,441 source orders, 96,470 delivered
orders and 93,350 customers with delivered orders. The first-delivered cohort
query found 357,413 eligible customer-month snapshots for 79,594 customers,
including 2,686 second-purchase events (0.752%). These are pre-run cohort
audit counts; final rebuilt dataset counts and partition distributions must
be taken from the published v5 model metadata.

The automated validation requires at least 300,000 rows, 70,000 customers
and 2,000 positive events to prevent accidental use of the earlier,
truncated third-or-later-purchase cohort.

## Evaluation protocol

Temporal CV is grouped by snapshot date and purges 90-day label overlap.
The model family is selected by second-purchase PR-AUC. Platt calibration
uses purged out-of-fold training predictions only. Validation selects the
diagnostic F2 threshold. The final chronological holdout is evaluated once.

The v5 report includes ranking and baseline PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, thresholded
precision/recall/F1/F2, balanced accuracy, Matthews correlation, Brier score,
log-loss, expected calibration error, top-5/10/20% monthly campaign capacity
metrics for both the selected approach and the RFM baselines, customer-cluster
bootstrap uncertainty, and separate results for customers absent from
training and validation. The ML model must earn selection against the
recency-only, recent-frequency-only and lifetime-frequency-only scorers.

## Limitations

- Olist is historical; no live data or online monitoring is implied.
- A good ranking metric does not prove the campaign causes extra purchases.
  Randomized experiments are required to establish incremental lift.
- Precision@K depends on campaign size and is not a fixed model property.
- F2 is a diagnostic threshold objective, not an economic objective.
- A production deployment needs approved contact capacity, customer consent,
  intervention cost, a minimum precision target, monitoring and randomized
  impact evaluation.
